# Governance

**Status: DESIGN PROPOSAL**

This document describes how decisions are made in the Intent-Centric Computing project and how that process is expected to evolve.

## Current state

The project is maintained by its initial author(s). Decisions are made through issues and pull requests on the public repository. All significant reasoning is documented in writing.

## Principles

- **Transparency.** Decisions are made in public and documented with rationale. No decisions happen in private channels that affect the project's direction.

- **Alignment with foundations.** Technical direction follows the foundational principles established in MANIFESTO.md. Proposals that contradict those principles require the foundational change process described below.

- **Community input valued.** Major changes are discussed publicly before adoption. The goal is informed consensus, not unilateral decree.

- **No capture.** No single entity — commercial or individual — may unilaterally alter the foundational principles. The project serves its users and community, not any particular organization's interests.

- **Human authority preserved.** The project's relationship to AI must remain that of a tool governed by the user, never an autonomous authority. This principle applies to the project's own governance as well: AI systems may assist in research and drafting, but decisions rest with human participants.

## Decision-making process (provisional)

### Minor decisions

Maintainer discretion. Examples: typo fixes, clarifications, minor restructuring. Documented in the PR description.

### Significant decisions

Require an RFC (Request for Comments) issue open for a minimum of 14 days. Examples: new design documents, changes to architectural proposals, addition of major research directions. The issue must describe the proposal, rationale, and tradeoffs. Silence is not consent — active agreement from at least one other contributor is needed.

### Foundational decisions

Changes to core principles (MANIFESTO.md), governance structure (this document), or licensing require broad community discussion and explicit consensus. There is no shortcut for these changes. The bar is deliberately high because these decisions constrain everything else.

## Future evolution

As the community grows, governance will likely evolve to include:

- A small steering group (3-5 people) with documented responsibilities
- An open RFC process with clear timelines and decision criteria
- Defined roles for maintainers, reviewers, and contributors

The specific structure will be determined by the community that forms. Premature formalization is avoided intentionally.

## Anti-capture provisions

- No single company or individual may hold veto power over the project's foundational direction.
- Governance changes require the same process as foundational changes (broad discussion, explicit consensus).
- If the project's governance is ever captured by a single interest, the open license ensures the community can fork and continue the work independently.
